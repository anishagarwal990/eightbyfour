## What Changes Between a Kitchen Spec and a Wardrobe Spec

Wardrobes carry less moisture risk than kitchens but more visible surface area — shutters, especially in sliding or wardrobe-door configurations, are what a room actually sees every day. That shifts the priority from moisture grade toward finish quality and hardware smoothness.

## Recommended Products

- **Carcass:** 19mm MR-grade plywood — see [Plywood](/products/plywood).
- **Shutters:** HDHMR or plywood finished in laminate or veneer — see [MDF and HDHMR](/products/mdf-and-hdhmr), [Laminates](/products/laminates) and [Veneers](/products/veneers).
- **Bonding:** Standard wood adhesive for lamination — see [Adhesives](/products/adhesive).

## Recommended Brands

Century and Greenply both carry the MR-grade plywood range used for wardrobe carcasses; Wigwam Excel is a common value-tier option for the same application.

## Material Combinations

| Look | Shutter finish | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimal / painted | HDHMR, painted or matte laminate | Smooth, chip-resistant surface |
| Textured / wood-grain | Veneer on plywood | Real wood grain, needs careful handling |
| High-gloss | Acrylic or gloss laminate | Shows fingerprints — best on low-touch panels |

## Typical BOQ

A standard 8×7 ft wardrobe run typically needs 4–5 sheets of 19mm plywood for the carcass and shelving, plus 2–3 sheets of laminate or veneer for shutter faces, sliding or hinged hardware, and adhesive.

## Buying Tips

For sliding wardrobe doors, confirm the shutter substrate can take the weight of your chosen sliding hardware before finalising material — heavier veneer or glass-faced shutters need a stiffer backing than standard laminate-on-HDHMR.
