## Why Kitchens Need a Different Spec Than the Rest of the House

A kitchen sees more moisture, more daily wear and more hardware load than any other room in the house — which is why the material choices here shouldn't be copy-pasted from the wardrobe or TV unit BOQ. Carcasses need boil-proof bonding, shutters need a finish that survives grease and wiping, and countertops need to resist heat and staining.

## Recommended Products

- **Carcass:** 19mm Boil Board (BWP-grade plywood) — take a look at our [Boil Boards](/products/boil-boards) range.
- **Shutters:** HDHMR or 19mm BWP plywood, finished in laminate — see [MDF and HDHMR](/products/mdf-and-hdhmr) and [Laminates](/products/laminates).
- **Countertop:** Acrylic solid surface for a seamless, joint-free run — browse [Corian / Acrylic Solid Surface](/products/corian-acrylic-solid-surface).
- **Bonding:** Fevicol synthetic resin adhesive for laminate lamination — see [Adhesives](/products/adhesive).

## Recommended Brands

## Typical BOQ

A standard 10×8 ft L-shaped kitchen typically runs 6–8 sheets of 19mm BWP plywood for the carcass, 3–4 sheets of laminate for shutter faces, plus adhesive, edge banding and hardware (hinges, channels, handles) — share your exact layout and we'll size the BOQ precisely.

## Buying Tips

Don't cut the BWP-grade corner on the carcass to save on the shutter finish — carcass failure from moisture is the expensive mistake, not the visible shutter material. Confirm hinge and channel brand compatibility with your fabricator before finalising hardware quantities.
