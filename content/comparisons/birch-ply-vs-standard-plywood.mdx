## Spec Comparison

| | Birch Ply (Baltic / Russian) | Standard Commercial Plywood |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core | 100% birch veneer, uniform layers, void-free | Mixed hardwood/softwood core, can have gaps between layers |
| Edge finish | Clean, sandable, can be left exposed without edge tape | Uneven layers, usually needs edge banding or tape |
| Screw & nail holding | Strong — threads bite into solid veneer throughout | Variable — softer or gapped core layers hold less |
| CNC / laser suitability | High — consistent density, smooth cut edges | Lower — voids and grain inconsistency can chip during cutting |
| Surface | Fully calibrated, wave-free | Calibration varies by brand and grade |
| Typical cost | Higher | Lower |
| Typical use | Furniture carcasses, drawer boxes, CNC components, jigs, exposed-edge pieces | Structural panels, sheathing, surfaces fully covered by laminate/veneer |

## The Short Version

Choose birch ply where the ply edge is visible in the finished piece, or where CNC precision and screw-holding matter — drawer boxes, cabinet carcasses left unlaminated on the inside, jigs, die boards. Choose standard plywood where the panel is fully covered by laminate, veneer, or paint on every face and edge — the void-free core isn't doing any visible work there, so it's not worth paying for.
