## Why Merino's Finish Range Matters More Than the Shade Name

Two cabinets in the exact same "Walnut" shade can look completely different once you factor in finish — one in Hi Gloss, reflective and formal; the other in Suede, soft and matte. Merino treats finish as a real design variable, not an afterthought, which is why the same decor code often ships in a handful of distinct textures. Picking a shade is really only half the decision — the finish is what determines how it actually behaves in a kitchen, a wardrobe, or a feature wall.

Below is a breakdown of Merino's finish vocabulary, grouped by what they're actually built for.

## Natural Wood-Grain Finishes

These reproduce the visual and tactile character of real timber, for designs meant to read as authentic wood rather than an obvious laminate.

- **FineWood (FNW)** — a two-level wood pore: a fine allover structure meeting a wider, low-gloss graining.
- **Pine Sync (PSC)** — a visual and haptic finish built to sit as close to a real wood effect as a laminate gets.
- **Horizon (HRZ)** — horizontal strokes of wood grain.
- **Monte (MNT)** — the rich colours and patterns characteristic of walnut.
- **Valley (VLY)** — a wooden finish featuring full-crowned grain.
- **Veneer (VNR)** — replicates the natural finish of wood grain directly.
- **Venza (VNZ)** — a natural finish built from half-crowned wood patterns.
- **Impression (IMP)** — authentic impressions of tree bark.
- **Streak (STK)** — a linear finish that reproduces straight wood formation.
- **Saw Cut (SCT)** — duplicates the look of wood cut by a bark saw.
- **Parrish (PRH)** — asymmetrical waves for a rustic wood impression.

## Gloss & Reflective Finishes

Where a design is meant to read as premium and formal, at the cost of showing fingerprints more readily.

- **Hi Gloss (HGL)** — a highly reflective, shiny surface.
- **Jewel (JW)** — inspired by a single crystal structure cut into flat facets.
- **Opal (OPL)** — borrows the look of melted glass.
- **Gemstone (GMS)** — gloss hints layered against a super-matte base.

## Matte & Soft-Touch Finishes

The practical choice for high-touch, daily-use surfaces — these hide scratches, smudges and wear far better than gloss.

- **Matt (MT)** — a flat, non-reflective, opaque surface.
- **Suede (SF)** — a soft-touch matte texture similar to suede fabric.
- **Feather Touch (FT)** — a royal matt with a smooth, creamy touch.
- **Pebble Touch (PT)** — an ultra-smooth, natural finish with near-zero light reflectivity.
- **Nubuck Leather (NL)** — an elegant finish that mimics the texture of man-made leather.

## Textured & Tactile Finishes

Finishes with real surface relief you can feel, not just see.

- **Fluted (FLT)** — two gloss levels and tactile depth in a straight fluted visual.
- **Woven (WVN)** — a thick, fabric-like weave across two gloss levels; dry, deep and tactile.
- **Slant (SLT)** — straight geometric lines intersecting in diverse directions.
- **Grains (GNR)** — micro-dots that reproduce a grain texture.
- **Microlines (ML)** — fine lines with very little gap between them.
- **Vertiline (VL)** — a multitude of fine vertical lines.
- **Fabric (FB)** — ripples that imitate the soft movement of woven fabric.
- **Linen (LNN)** — soft, smooth movements that mimic linen.

## Artistic & Pattern Finishes

More expressive, less literal — built for feature panels and accent surfaces rather than whole-kitchen runs.

- **Chimera (CMR)** — the impression created by fire.
- **Crossfire (CFR)** — an artistic representation of gunfire from two or more directions.
- **Dew Drop (DW)** — delicate patterns capturing the freshness of morning dew.
- **Drizzle (DZL)** — a progressive dash-like structure imitating raindrops on a window.
- **Flicker (FK)** — horizontal ripples emulating flickering light.
- **Wave (WV)** — free-flowing patterns resembling moving water.
- **Bark (BRK)** — inspired by the outermost layers of stems and roots.
- **Brush (BR)** — strokes that replicate a paintbrush effect.
- **Cement (CMT)** — a rustic effect inspired by concrete walls.
- **Moonscape (MNS)** — a rugged, weathered-stone look with a natural, warm touch.
- **Whitney (WTN)** — a classic, well-defined etched texture.

## How to Actually Use This

Match the finish to how the surface gets used, not just how it looks in a sample:

- **Kitchen shutters and high-touch surfaces** — lean toward Suede, Matt, Pebble Touch or Feather Touch; they hide daily wear.
- **Feature walls and low-touch accent panels** — Hi Gloss, Jewel or the artistic finishes (Chimera, Crossfire, Wave) can carry more visual drama without the upkeep concern.
- **Wardrobe interiors** — textured finishes (Woven, Grains, Microlines) add tactile quality somewhere it's rarely touched directly, so gloss level matters less than for a kitchen.
