## Same Family, Different Moisture Tolerance

MDF and HDHMR are both engineered wood-fibre boards — smoother and more uniform than plywood, which is what makes them good for painted finishes and profile-routed shutter detailing. The decision between them almost always comes down to one variable: how much moisture the application will see.

## When to Choose MDF

Standard interior-grade MDF is the right call for applications that stay dry: painted wall panelling, false ceiling trims, decorative mouldings, and furniture in rooms without humidity exposure. It machines cleanly and takes paint or laminate finish evenly.

## When to Choose HDHMR

HDHMR (high-density, high-moisture-resistant board) costs more but tolerates the humidity that standard MDF can't — wardrobe shutters (especially near bathrooms), kitchen-adjacent cabinetry, and any profile-routed shutter face where you want both the machining quality of MDF and real moisture tolerance.

## The Practical Shortcut

If you're unsure which application you're speccing for, ask: will this board ever be in a room with a shower, a kitchen sink, or Hyderabad's monsoon humidity nearby? If yes, spec HDHMR. If the application is a dry living or bedroom space with no moisture exposure, standard MDF is a reasonable cost saving.
