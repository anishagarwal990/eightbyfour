## The Question Behind the Question

"Best plywood for kitchen" usually isn't really one question — it's three: what moisture grade, what thickness, and does it matter which brand. Get the first two right and the brand choice mostly comes down to what's actually in stock at trade pricing when you need it.

## Start With Moisture Grade

Kitchens are the single application in a home where skipping BWP grade most often causes real problems — delamination and swelling around the sink base, dishwasher housing, and any cabinet within splash range of the tap. [BWP (boil-water-proof) plywood](/products/boil-boards) is tested by boiling the bonded sheet, not just resisting occasional dampness the way MR-grade does.

**Rule of thumb:** BWP under the sink and around wet appliances, MR-grade is acceptable for upper cabinets and pantry storage away from water — but if your budget allows it, standardising on BWP throughout the kitchen removes the guesswork and future-proofs the cabinet against a plumbing leak you didn't plan for.

## Then Thickness

- **19mm** — carcasses and shutters that need to hold hinges and channels securely. This is the standard for structural kitchen panels.
- **12mm** — shelves, cabinet backs and lower-load internal panels.
- **6mm** — drawer bottoms and infill panels where load is minimal.

Going thinner than these to save cost usually shows up later as sagging shelves or hardware pulling loose — it's not a corner worth cutting on a kitchen.

## Does Brand Matter?

Once grade and thickness are locked, brand differences (Century, Green Panel and others in our [Plywood](/products/plywood) catalogue) come down to consistency of bonding quality and whatever your fabricator has the most experience working with. If you don't have a strong preference, ask what's in stock — sourcing exactly what's specified beats waiting on a preferred brand that's backordered.
